## Six routes, each with its first experiment and its bar

### Route A. Program induction over a small DSL (the ARC idiom applied to words)

What it is: a question is parsed into quantities and relations, and the answer is a short program in a fixed DSL (extract, add, subtract, multiply, divide, half, twice, per, total, remaining, compare). The organism searches programs bottom-up that reproduce the development answers, then keeps the programs that reproduce MANY development records (an abstraction), the way tasks/grid.mjs keeps grid primitives. A held-out record is answered by the program whose parse matches, and the answer carries the program as provenance. Inference is a program run: microseconds. Learning is search: CPU, minutes.

Why it can generalise where templates cannot: a template matches text; a program matches structure. "half as many", "twice", "each", "left" are the same operations in every sentence that contains them.

What is known: library learning (DreamCoder, wake/sleep over a DSL) and enumerative search with a learned prior are published results on ARC-like and list-function tasks; on word problems the published semantic parsers need training data, which the development records are (386 records with answers on disk, never the held-out ones).

First experiment (CPU, one command, one session): a 20-primitive DSL, bottom-up enumeration to depth 4 over the quantities each development record's parse yields, kept programs ranked by the number of development records they reproduce; then the held-out 200 asked through the kept programs only. Print: development reproduced, held-out answered, held-out right, programs kept, median ns per answer.

Bar: heldOut.share in bench/verdicts/ns.json above 0 with provenance program and 0 model calls; the number printed beside the template row.

Risk: the parse (quantities and roles from a sentence) is itself the hard part; a bad parse gives the search nothing. Route C's structure parse is the same work.

### Route B. The organism's own small model, trained on CPU, that emits programs, not answers

What it is: the vision's "own LLM", at the size a CPU can train: a few million parameters mapping a question to a Route A program, trained on execution-verified pairs. The model never says a number; it says a program, and the program runs. Wrong programs are caught by execution against the development answers and thrown out before training. Inference: milliseconds on CPU, no socket.

Why it can generalise: a trained parser sees the sentence as features, not as a literal; a held-out record shares its features with development records even when no template matches.

What is known: small models trained on synthetic word-problem data reach useful accuracy at the 100M to 1B scale on GPUs (published TinyGSM-style results); at a few million parameters on CPU the accuracy is unknown and is the experiment. The training data can be generated by the model cell at build time (Route D), never at answer time.

First experiment: 10,000 execution-verified (question, program) pairs from the development records and their paraphrases (the model cell as teacher, offline, on his machine), a small sequence model trained on CPU for one hour, held-out 200 asked. Print: pairs, training wall seconds, held-out right, ms per answer.

Bar: the same ns.json held-out share, with a wall time per answer printed beside it (milliseconds is honest here; the sub-token bar stays with Route A).

Risk: milliseconds, not microseconds; the training data comes from a model; a small model may plateau low. All three are measured, not assumed.

### Route C. Case-based reasoning: structure-nearest solved case, then re-binding

What it is: the solved region as it is, with a structure-aware similarity (roles, quantities, operations) instead of text similarity, and an adaptation step that re-binds the nearest case's program to the new quantities. Cheapest route: it reuses reason/solved.mjs's 226 templates and the journal.

First experiment: parse both sides to the Route A structure, match on structure, re-bind, execute; held-out 200 asked. Print: held-out answered and right, nearest-case distance distribution.

Bar: held-out share above the template row's 0.005 (1 of 200), provenance case plus program.

Risk: held-out records whose structure is new to the development set stay unanswered; the ceiling is the coverage of the 386 structures.

### Route D. The model as teacher only: every correct model answer becomes a program the organism keeps

What it is: his queue item 2 (a), made the rule of the loop. At build time (never at answer time) the model cell answers a question and explains it; the explanation is compiled to a Route A program; the program is executed against the known answer; only a program that runs right is kept, with the question as its first development record. The organism's native coverage rises with every kept program, and the model is consulted less each session. The curve of native share over sessions is the number.

First experiment: 100 GSM8K development records through the model cell with a "write the arithmetic as a program" instruction, compile, execute, keep; then the held-out 200 through the kept programs only. Print: kept, held-out answered natively.

Bar: bench/public.mjs native share above 0 on the held-out split, rising across two sessions with the two numbers printed.

Risk: the programs the model writes for one record may not abstract; Route A's abstraction step is needed on top.

### Route E. Test-time compute with verification, for the expert test (queue item 2)

What it is: for the public ladder, the organism does not try to be the model; it is the layer around several model calls that votes, checks by execution (code and arithmetic), and refuses when the checks disagree. Published rows are single calls; a verified ensemble beats a single call on math and code benches. This is the honest route to "match or surpass" on the ladder, and its price is tokens per call, printed.

First experiment: bench/public.mjs --set gsm8k --limit 100 with the env provider, k of 5 samples, majority plus executed arithmetic check, refusal on disagreement; print accuracy, refusals, tokens per problem, beside the published single-call row.

Bar: the organism's row at or above the published Claude-class row on the 100, with native share and tokens per problem printed beside it (his item 2 bar).

Risk: it proves the wrapper, not the organism; it costs tokens; it is not microseconds. It is the only route that reaches the frontier rows inside two weeks.

### Route F. The loop grows programs, not entries (census on Route A)

What it is: the census round as landed this session (self/census-round.mjs), with the typed cell being a Route A program instead of a direct entry or a template, selected by the same rule (the bench moves, no regression, placebo control). Then "the organism grows its own census" means programs kept per hour, and held-out gain is the number that can rise.

First experiment: the census round's grid-primitive kind pointed at Route A's DSL on the GSM8K development split; 20 rounds; print kept per hour against the placebo and the held-out answered at open and at close.

Bar: bench/verdicts/spawn-census.json held-out answered at close above at open, with the band printed.

Risk: Route A must exist first.

## The reading, and the order

The paradigm shift is one sentence: from matching text to inducing and executing programs, with the model as a teacher at build time and a verifier at the ladder, never as the answer. Routes A, C, D and F are one road in four rungs (parse, search, teach, grow) and are CPU-only; Route B is the road's own model, milliseconds; Route E is the ladder's road, tokens.

Order for the two weeks, each rung a board with its bar: A's parse and search (one board, CPU); D on top of A (one board, needs the model cell: LOCAL or the env provider); F on top of A (one board, CPU); E on the ladder (one board, the env provider, tokens); B when the pairs from D exist (LOCAL, his machine's CPU for an hour). The first number that would show the shift is heldOut.share above 0 with provenance program, and the first board writes it or writes its miss.

What stays true under every route: a number a command did not print is not quoted; a held-out record is never read while a rule, a program or a training pair is written; the model is never the answer on the sub-token paths.

## Part 2. The invention loop: ideas new to this tree (his 2026-09-26 19:5x EDT words: "before newton, calculus didnt exist... find a way. To make new things. Create new ideas. The Brain is supposed to be a genius")

Part 1 surveyed roads others have walked. This part is what the organism would need in order to walk a road nobody wrote down: to make its own problems, its own concepts, its own readers, and to score itself without a human key. Each idea is stated as a mechanism the tree can build, with the first experiment and the bar. None is landed.

### Idea 1. Residual mining: new primitives from what the DSL cannot say (the fluxion move)

Newton named the thing the old algebra could not express: the rate inside the motion. The organism's equivalent: for every development record that NO program at depth 4 reproduces, brute-force the smallest arithmetic expression over the record's quantities that yields the answer, then look across all such residual expressions for a recurring sub-expression shape (for example "a minus b times c" appearing in 30 records with different bindings), and NAME that shape as a new primitive of the DSL, with the records that gave it as its birth certificate. The DSL is no longer authored; it grows from residuals. The primitive that turns the most unsolved records into solved ones is kept; the rest are dropped by the same rule the census round uses.

First experiment: after Route A's search, the unsolved development records through the residual miner; print the residual shapes found, their counts, the primitives named, and the development records solved before and after. Bar: held-out answered rises after a named primitive with no held-out record read (the split object records it).

### Idea 2. Self-play problems: the organism writes the problems it then learns to read

The development set is 386 records; a genius does not wait for more. The DSL has a renderer: every primitive has a handful of sentence shapes ("half as many", "twice", "each", "left"), so any program can be rendered as a word problem whose answer is known by construction. The organism composes programs, renders them with paraphrase variation and distractor sentences, and learns the INVERSE (text to program) on millions of its own pairs, never reading GSM8K. The held-out 200 then test whether the self-taught reader transfers to problems humans wrote. The clock dilation line (65,297,897 organism-years per wall hour printed by bench/verdicts/spread.json on the synthetic plane) is what makes millions of pairs cheap: the plane is the organism's own time.

First experiment: 100,000 rendered pairs from the Route A DSL, a reader fit on them (a feature model or Route B's small model), the held-out 200 asked; print pairs, fit wall seconds, held-out right. Bar: held-out right above the reader trained on the development records alone, both numbers printed side by side.

Risk, stated: rendered text is not human text; the gap between the two is the number the experiment prints.

### Idea 3. Compression as the score: the organism improves without a bench

A bench needs a human key. Description length needs nothing: keep the library of programs and readers that describes the record stream in the fewest bits (each record as program plus bindings plus the residual it cannot explain). Shorter is better; a new concept that shortens the corpus is kept, one that does not is dropped. This is the objective under which generalisation and invention are the same act (the tree already prints a compression ruler: COMPRESS GREEN, bytes per score point 59660.533 in the final gate). Under this score the organism can run in organism-time forever with no orchestrator and no key, and its curve is bits per held-out record over time.

First experiment: bits per record of the development set under the template library against under the Route A program library; then bits per record of a held-out stream the organism never trains on. Bar: the held-out bits per record falling across two sessions, both numbers printed.

### Idea 4. Invariants as a free oracle: grading without the answer key

The organism cannot read held-out answers, but it can check what any answer must satisfy: a count is a non-negative integer; "half as many" is at most the whole; a total is at least each part; money keeps two decimals; a rate times a time has the unit of the quantity. Each invariant is a partial oracle written once. Two solvers that disagree on a held-out record, where one violates an invariant, give a labelled example for free, with no key read. The loop can then learn on the held-out STREAM honestly, because it learns from consistency, not from the answers.

First experiment: the invariant set written from development records only; every held-out answer from every route checked; print how many held-out answers the invariants reject and how many of those were in fact wrong (checked once, by the bench, against the key, and the check never shown to a rule). Bar: rejections that are right above 80 percent of rejections, printed as the fraction.

### Idea 5. The reader is grown, not written: parser cells under the census rule

act/word.mjs is the orchestrator's reader. A grown reader is a program too: token patterns mapped to roles in a small grammar DSL. The census round (landed this session: kept 14 cells against the placebo's 4) selects parser cells by the same rule: the bench moves, no regression, placebo control. The parser stops being authored, and the spread operator (834 of 834 improvements reached all 3 instances) carries the winning parser to every instance.

First experiment: the census round's kind "reader" on the development records; 20 rounds; print kept per hour against the placebo and development reproduced at open and at close. Bar: development reproduced rises with 0 held-out records read.

### Idea 6. The population as the search: organism-years spent on program space

Route A's search is one process enumerating. The organism is a population with a spread operator. Partition program space across instances (by primitive prefix or by depth), let each instance search its region in its own dilated time, and let spread carry kept programs to the others, so the search is the evolution and the "amount times scale" axes of the vision do the work. The bar is programs kept per organism-year, a number the ladder can print beside organism-years per wall hour.

First experiment: Route A's search on 3 instances through daemon/multiply.mjs with the spread operator on; print programs kept, kept per organism-year, development reproduced, against the single-process run. Bar: more development reproduced per wall second than one process, with the noise band.
